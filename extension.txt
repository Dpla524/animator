Ink Courier is a game played on top of whatever drawing the animator is revealing. The ink of the current program is the level. A bright spark is the player, and it has to stay on lines that have already been drawn. Click Ride, then click the drawing so the canvas has the keyboard. The 2× button beside Ride doubles the spark, the enemies, the bolts, the dash, the powerup timer, the teleport wait, and the score. While riding, Pause or the Esc key freezes the spark, the enemies, the powerup timer, the teleport wait, and the ink until Resume or Esc. The drawing's own speed slider stays as it is when the ride is moving. The arrow keys ride the spark along the line under it that runs in that direction. If that line does not run that way, one press teleports the spark straight across onto the next parallel line in that direction. A shorter stroke off to the side does not count, so on a wide loop such as the track the hop reaches the far side instead of a corner. The spark streaks across the gap, with a fading trail and a ring where it will land, and then another teleport waits five seconds. The status line counts that wait down. Up from the middle of the spiral moves onto the line above, and the same press crosses from one street to the next on a grid such as blocks. At a corner, holding the key that heads along a new branch turns the spark onto that branch as the vertex arrives, and the key still follows the branch that actually heads that way instead of jumping onto a line that heads somewhere else. Riding through a meeting point, such as the origin of the string art, stays on the ink and does not skip past that point. On a branching drawing such as the tree, the spark stays on its twig and turns only onto a stroke that meets that twig, rather than jumping across to a nearby branch.

The spacebar dashes. During a dash the spark moves much faster along the ink and takes one health from an enemy only when that enemy is on the same line and inside a small reach. One dash hits each enemy only once. After a dash finishes, space will not dash again for 0.35 seconds, or 0.25 seconds once the score has reached 500. Those enemies are mites. Each mite rides the continuous ink toward the spark and follows every joint of the shape. It arrives at a corner, or where one stroke ends on another, and continues from that same point, without skipping ahead and without jumping a gap. When two connected routes both reach the spark, the mite takes the shorter one, so a shortcut such as the middle drops on the switchback is used instead of running the long way around. New mites appear on any revealed ink outside a wide circle around the spark, so one does not show up beside the spark and a new one is not always sent down the same stroke. If every line is already inside that circle, a mite still appears at the farthest point that is clear of the spark. They move more slowly than the spark slides. Touching an enemy costs a life only when that enemy is on the spark's line. Enemies on other lines do not hit the spark, and the spark cannot hit them. The spark is safe for a little over two seconds after a hit, and a dash is safe too. The spark starts with five lives, and the count never rises above five. A green heart appears on the revealed ink only while fewer than five lives remain. It shows up every so often, away from the spark, the same way as the mint mark, and only one heart is out at a time. Riding over it gives one life back. Losing the last life ends the run. Each drawing keeps its own top five finished scores. A later run is added only when it beats the lowest of those five. The list for the drawing that is open stays in a box at the top-right corner, including after the page is reopened. Switching drawings shows that drawing's list. If that run places on the list, a light sweeps around the border of the box. Press space after that to ride again, or press Stop and then Ride.

The score gains one point per second while the spark is riding ink. A pink mite has one health and is worth 10 points when defeated. Once the bolt unlocks at 500 points, an orange enemy with two health appears now and then among the pink mites. After 1000 points, violet enemies with three health also appear on the ink outside that circle, and the orange ones show up more often. They ride the same continuous path and are worth 20 and 30 points. A hit turns the violet enemy into the orange two-health sprite, and the next hit turns that into the pink one-health mite. Every 1000 points a larger blue boss with 20 health spawns and rides the ink too. Its health bar sits under the score. It keeps the blue shape for the whole fight, while the smaller enemies still change sprite as they lose health. Defeating that boss is worth 100 points, and if a boss is already out, the next one waits until it falls. A dash takes one health from each enemy it reaches, and a bolt takes one health from the first enemy it reaches before disappearing. Powers arrive every 250 points up to 1000. At 250 a dash lasts longer, 0.4 seconds instead of 0.28. At 500 a dash also fires a bolt along the line in that direction, and the wait after a dash is a little shorter. The bolt looks the same on every stroke, including a vertical one. It takes one health from the first enemy it reaches and then disappears. It keeps going through a vertex whenever the ink continues in a straight line, so a shot along a street travels that whole stretch and reaches the far end. At 750 the same dash fires a second bolt back the other way. At 1000 a gold spiky flare spins around the spark, and once a bolt reaches the end of its straight stretch it reflects onto the connected stroke. When that next stroke is the only one, as on each fold of the braid, the bolt keeps reflecting through the rest of that path, including a fold sharper than a right angle, and it will not ride the same stroke twice. When several strokes leave the joint, it still takes only one of them. At 2000 points the bolt becomes a disk. The disk flies out along the ink and passes through up to ten enemies before it turns around, then it rides the ink back to the spark. Firing stays on the dash, so the next disk can be thrown while an earlier one is still out. The pair from the mint mark and the backward shot are disks too. After 1000 points a mint mark also appears on the revealed ink every so often, away from the spark, and only one mark is out at a time. Riding over it makes each bolt a pair for 10 seconds, so a dash that would fire one bolt in a direction fires two, the second just behind the first. The backward bolt from 750 is doubled the same way. The next mark waits until that boost has been used for a while. Reaching 500 points still wins. The ride pauses and the status line offers space to keep riding, after which the score continues past 500 so the later powers can be used. At most six mites share a small drawing at the start. A shape that covers more of the page, which is what extending the shape slider does, allows more at once. The cap also rises as the score climbs, up to twenty-four. Past 1000 that ceiling keeps rising one place at a time, and new enemies arrive more slowly than they did when the limit used to vanish, so the crowd builds over a long ride instead of flooding in. Past 6000 a wave can bring two at once, and past 12000 it can bring three. The mix keeps shifting away from pink mites toward orange two-health enemies and then violet three-health enemies, so a long ride is mostly the violet ones. If the drawing has not revealed any ink yet, the game waits instead of punishing the player. Leaving the ink does not score. Editing the program, moving the shape slider, or changing the playback speed still updates the drawing underneath, and the spark simply rides whatever ink is currently on the canvas. Running the playback backward erases ink. An enemy whose stroke disappears, so that it is no longer standing on a line, is removed. That removal does not add to the score. Stopping the game clears the spark, the bolts, and the mites and leaves the animation as it was.
